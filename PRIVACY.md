# Snapfly Privacy Policy

*Last updated: October 20, 2026*

Snapfly hangs your screenshots on a line at the top of the screen. Everything it
does happens on your Mac, unless you set up the AI assistant with your own key
or turn on syncing with your iPhone (below).

- **No data is collected.** Snapfly has no account, no analytics, no tracking
  and no advertising. It does not send your screenshots, their text or anything
  about you to the developer or to anyone else, except a file you choose to
  share as a link (below).
- **Nothing leaves your Mac on its own.** Reading the text in a screenshot,
  covering private details, translating and describing all run on your Mac,
  with Apple's frameworks.
- **The time stamp.** While Stamp the date and time is on, Snapfly asks the
  atomic clock of the PTB, Germany's national metrology institute
  (ptbtime1.ptb.de), for the exact time of each new screenshot, the way a clock
  sets itself (NTP). Only that question goes out, with nothing about you or your
  screenshots. With the stamp off and no AI assistant set up, Snapfly never
  connects to the internet, except when you choose Check for Updates…, which
  downloads the newest version from GitHub and sends nothing about you.
- **The AI assistant, only if you set it up.** In Settings › AI Assistant you
  may give Snapfly your own API key for Claude (Anthropic) or ChatGPT (OpenAI).
  The key stays in your Mac's keychain. When you save it, open that pane or
  choose Check Again, Snapfly asks Anthropic or OpenAI, with the key, for the
  list of models it may use, to see that the key works; nothing else goes
  with it. Then, only when you choose Write a Reply,
  Explain or Order Details for a screenshot, Snapfly sends that screenshot,
  with its private details covered as for an AI chat, and what you typed,
  straight from your Mac to Anthropic or OpenAI. With Find more with Claude
  (or ChatGPT) turned on under Privacy, it also sends the words of each
  screenshot, to find private details the patterns miss; it is off unless you
  turn it on. On the iPhone the same holds, set up in More › AI Assistant,
  with the key in the iPhone's keychain: only when you tap Explain in
  Protect Picture, Snapfly sends the protected copy of that picture, never
  the original, and the questions you ask about it, straight from the iPhone.
  Requests to OpenAI ask it not to store them for later retrieval. What they
  do with it is set by their own privacy policies and your account with them;
  they bill each request to that account. Nothing of it goes to the developer.
- **Your own details, only if you add them.** Under Always Hide, on the
  Mac and the iPhone, you may add your name, your address, bank
  cards and bank accounts, so Snapfly covers them in the ways they are
  written. They are kept in the keychain of the Mac or iPhone you add them
  on, on that device alone and never in iCloud. A backup of the iPhone
  holds them only sealed to that same iPhone, so no other device can open
  them. Settings shows a card's or an account's number by its last four
  digits. They are never sent, never written to a log, and before the words
  of a screenshot go to the AI assistant, with Find more with Claude (or
  ChatGPT) turned on, every one of your details in them is hidden, and so
  is what your own rules cover.
- **Share links, only when you choose Copy Link.** Snapfly then uploads that
  one photo, as it leaves the line with its private details covered, or that
  recording or PDF, to Snapfly's link service on Cloudflare (Cloudflare R2, in
  the developer's account), and copies its link. Anyone with the link can see
  the file for seven days; then it is deleted for good, and Delete Link
  deletes it sooner. No name, account or address is kept with it; the service
  sees your internet address only to stop too many uploads at once, and does
  not store it. The links you made, with the token that deletes each one, are
  kept on your Mac.
- **Syncing with your iPhone, only if you turn it on.** With Sync on in
  Settings on the Mac and under More › Mac and iCloud on the iPhone,
  Snapfly keeps them in step through
  your own iCloud, in the private database of your Apple account, which only
  your devices signed in to it can read. What goes is the copy that leaves the
  line, with its private details covered, a small preview of it, and facts
  about it: its name, when it was taken, what kinds of details were covered,
  never the covered words. Originals go only if you choose Originals too, and
  only those taken from then on. The contents of each record are encrypted
  with your iCloud keys; the pictures are encrypted by Apple in iCloud, and end
  to end only if you turned on Advanced Data Protection. Nothing of it goes to
  the developer or through a server of Snapfly's. Turn Sync off and nothing
  more goes; what is in iCloud stays until you remove Snapfly's data in your
  iCloud settings.
- **Pairing with a code, for another Apple account.** When an iPhone on a
  different Apple account types the code your Mac shows, and you say yes on
  the Mac, your Mac shares its synced screenshots with that account alone,
  through iCloud: that iPhone sees what your Mac syncs, and what it sends
  hangs on your line. To meet, the two leave short notes in Snapfly's public
  iCloud database for a minute or so: the devices' names and an iCloud
  identifier that names no one, sealed with the code, and taken down again.
  Disconnect… on the Mac takes that account off the share.
- **Your files stay yours.** Snapfly reads screenshots in the folders you let it
  watch, and the photos and PDFs you bring to it yourself, and copies, sends or
  deletes them only when you ask it to. A photo or PDF you bring stays where it
  is, unchanged: what leaves the line is a copy, a photo without where and with
  what it was taken, a PDF drawn again page by page with the details you chose
  covered. Cover Files does the same for many files at once, saving each copy
  in a new folder you choose; it reads them on your Mac, unless Find more with
  Claude or ChatGPT is on under Privacy, which sends the words of each.
- **Permissions.** Accessibility access lets Snapfly paste into the app you are
  using when you send a screenshot. While you choose what a screenshot takes,
  smart selection uses it to ask the app under the pointer where a picture or
  a button sits there, to outline it: only where things are, and nothing of
  it leaves your Mac. While you record a guide, it asks the app you click what
  was clicked there, below. Screen Recording lets it take the
  screenshots and recordings you ask for, with ⌃⌥S, the screenshot button,
  Record Screen or Record a Guide. So that the send button shows only on chat websites, it asks
  the browser in front for the address of the open page, once you allow it,
  and reads nothing else. While the time stamp is on, that address is also
  written on the stamp of a new screenshot, which stays on your Mac. Settings › Permissions shows each of these, and you
  can turn any off at any time in System Settings.
- **Guides.** While you record a guide, Snapfly takes a screenshot of the
  screen at each click and asks the app you clicked, through Accessibility,
  what was clicked: its kind and its name, never what you typed. Snapfly does
  not record what you type. The guide and its screenshots stay on your Mac, in
  Snapfly's own folder, the last 20 of them, until you delete them. What you
  send or save from a guide has its private details covered.
- **The workspace folder, only if you turn it on.** In Settings › Workspace you
  may have Snapfly keep screenshots in a folder on your Desktop, with a folder
  for each kind. It stays on your Mac and is yours: Snapfly reads and writes
  only that folder, moves screenshots between its folders when you ask, and
  moves them to the Trash only when you choose Move to Trash or Delete…, which
  asks first. Turning it off or removing the app deletes nothing. If iCloud
  Drive keeps your Desktop, Apple copies the folder to iCloud, private details
  covered or not; that is Apple's service and your choice.
- **Calls.** While Not during a call is on, Snapfly asks the Mac whether the
  microphone is in use, so the line keeps away during a meeting and nothing
  of Snapfly shows in what you share. It hears nothing doing so.
- **What is read stays in memory, briefly.** To cover a screenshot again
  without reading it again, Snapfly keeps the text it read in the last few
  pictures in memory, never on disk, and forgets it when the iPhone app goes
  to the background, unless you turn on Faster picture processing (below). The iPhone app notes which screenshots you protected
  lately by their place in Photos alone, to show them again; nothing of what
  they show. A page scanned with the iPhone's camera stays in memory while
  the app is open and is never saved unless you save its covered copy, or
  the covered PDF of the pages, which holds pictures of the covered pages
  and no text; the camera is used only while you scan. A tap on an app
  under the pages or in Protect Picture puts the covered copy on the
  clipboard and opens that app, or its website; Snapfly sends nothing
  itself, and it reaches the app only when you paste it there. The share extension, which covers a
  picture shared to Snapfly from another app, works the same way, on the
  iPhone, and keeps nothing of the picture; what you chose to cover, the
  look and your own rules are kept for it in the iPhone's keychain, never a
  picture or a word read. How long each step of covering took is written to the device's
  log as numbers only, never a word of what was read.
- **Faster picture processing on the iPhone, only if you turn it on.** It is
  off until you turn it on in Settings, after a page that says what it does;
  allowing Snapfly to see your photos is not a yes to it. Then the iPhone
  reads your pictures from the last 60 days ahead of time: the screenshots
  only, every photo Snapfly may see, or only the photos you pick. The words
  read, where they are, and which kinds of details they hold are kept on the
  iPhone alone, in Snapfly's own folder, locked while the iPhone is and left
  out of backups. Nothing of it is sent anywhere, and photos only in iCloud
  are not downloaded for it. Pause stops it, Delete Local Index deletes
  everything prepared, and turning it off deletes it too.
- **Sound in recordings, only if you choose it.** Under Settings › General you
  may have screen recordings take the Mac's sound, your microphone, or both.
  macOS asks you before the microphone is used the first time. The sound is in
  the recording only, saved on your Mac like it, and goes nowhere else.
- **Studio notes how you moved, not what you typed.** While the screen is
  recorded, Snapfly notes where the pointer went, where it clicked and when a
  key went down, so Studio can zoom in there. Which key is never noted. It is
  kept on your Mac with the recording's Studio work, and goes once the
  recording has left the line and the history. Nothing of it is sent
  anywhere.

## Datenschutz (Deutsch)

Snapfly erhebt keine Daten. Die App hat kein Konto, keine Analyse, kein Tracking
und keine Werbung. Mit dem Internet verbindet sie sich von sich aus nur, solange der
Zeitstempel eingeschaltet ist, um die Atomuhr der PTB nach der Uhrzeit zu fragen.
Richtest du den KI-Assistenten mit deinem eigenen Schlüssel für Claude
(Anthropic) oder ChatGPT (OpenAI) ein, schickt Snapfly ein Bildschirmfoto mit
verdeckten privaten Angaben nur dann direkt an Anthropic oder OpenAI, wenn du
Antwort schreiben, Erklären oder Bestelldetails wählst, und die Wörter jedes
Bildschirmfotos nur, wenn du „Mehr finden mit Claude“ unter Datenschutz
einschaltest. Auf dem iPhone schickt „Erklären“ in „Bild schützen“ nur die
geschützte Kopie, nie das Original, und deine Fragen dazu, und nur, wenn du
darauf tippst. Der Schlüssel bleibt im Schlüsselbund deines Macs oder iPhones. Deine eigenen
Angaben unter „Immer verbergen“ auf dem Mac und dem iPhone, also Name,
Adresse, Bankkarten und Bankkonten, bleiben im Schlüsselbund des Geräts, auf dem du sie einträgst,
und nie in iCloud; ein Backup des iPhones enthält sie nur versiegelt für genau dieses iPhone, kein
anderes Gerät kann sie öffnen. Sie werden nie gesendet, und bevor die Wörter eines Bildschirmfotos
an den KI-Assistenten gehen, sind sie darin verdeckt, ebenso, was deine eigenen Regeln verdecken.
Texterkennung,
Abdecken privater Angaben, Übersetzen und Beschreiben laufen auf deinem Mac.
Schaltest du Synchronisieren auf dem Mac und dem iPhone ein, gleicht Snapfly
beide über dein eigenes iCloud ab, in der privaten Datenbank deines
Apple-Accounts: die Kopie, die die Leine verlässt, mit verdeckten privaten
Angaben, eine kleine Vorschau und Angaben dazu, nie die verdeckten Wörter.
Originale nur, wenn du „Auch Originale“ wählst, und nur die ab dann
aufgenommenen. Nichts davon geht an den Entwickler. Gibt ein iPhone mit einem
anderen Apple Account den Code ein, den dein Mac zeigt, und sagst du auf dem
Mac Ja, teilt dein Mac seine synchronisierten Bildschirmfotos über iCloud nur
mit diesem Account. Zum Verbinden liegen für etwa eine Minute kurze, mit dem
Code versiegelte Notizen in Snapflys öffentlicher iCloud-Datenbank: die Namen
der Geräte und eine iCloud-Kennung, die niemanden nennt.
Snapfly liest nur Bildschirmfotos in den Ordnern, die du freigibst, und Fotos und
PDFs, die du der App selbst gibst, und kopiert, sendet oder löscht sie nur, wenn
du es willst. Fotos verlassen die Leine ohne Aufnahmeort und Kameradaten.
Bildschirmaufnahmen macht es nur, wenn du darum bittest, und vom Browser liest
es nur die Adresse der offenen Seite, damit die Senden-Taste nur auf
Chat-Websites erscheint. Beim Aufnehmen einer Anleitung macht Snapfly bei jedem
Klick ein Bildschirmfoto und fragt die angeklickte App, was dort angeklickt
wurde, nie, was du tippst; die Anleitung bleibt auf deinem Mac. Ob das Mikrofon
gerade in Gebrauch ist, fragt Snapfly
nur, damit die Leine während eines Anrufs oben bleibt; es hört nichts.
Den gelesenen Text der letzten Bilder behält Snapfly kurz im Arbeitsspeicher,
nie auf der Festplatte, damit ein Bild nach einer geänderten Einstellung nicht
noch einmal gelesen werden muss; die iPhone-App vergisst ihn, sobald sie in den
Hintergrund geht. Welche Bildschirmfotos du zuletzt geschützt hast, merkt sich
die iPhone-App nur über ihre Kennung in Fotos, nichts von ihrem Inhalt. Eine
mit der Kamera des iPhones gescannte Seite bleibt nur im Arbeitsspeicher,
solange die App offen ist, und wird nie gesichert, außer du sicherst ihre
abgedeckte Kopie oder das abgedeckte PDF der Seiten, das nur Bilder der
abgedeckten Seiten enthält, keinen Text; die Kamera läuft nur, während du
scannst. Tippst du unter den Seiten oder in „Bild schützen“ auf eine App,
legt Snapfly die abgedeckte Kopie in die Zwischenablage und öffnet die App
oder ihre Website; Snapfly selbst sendet nichts, sie kommt erst dort an,
wenn du sie einsetzt. Die
Teilen-Erweiterung, die ein aus einer anderen App geteiltes Bild abdeckt,
arbeitet genauso auf dem iPhone und behält nichts vom Bild; was du abdecken
lässt, der Look und deine eigenen Regeln liegen für sie im Schlüsselbund des
iPhones, nie ein Bild oder ein gelesenes Wort. Wie
lange jeder Schritt des Abdeckens dauerte, steht nur als Zahl im Protokoll des
Geräts, nie ein gelesenes Wort.
